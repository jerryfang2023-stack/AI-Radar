The VC Read · Trace's Take

Engineers from both Anthropic and OpenAI writing personal angel checks into the same design startup is a more interesting signal than the institutional round itself -- when people building the underlying coding agents bet personally that the design layer around them is still an open problem, that's real conviction, not just access. The platform risk here is obvious and immediate: Paper has maybe 18 months before Figma or the coding-agent platforms themselves decide this feature belongs natively in their own product.
Analysis
Paper, a San Francisco startup building an AI-native design platform, raised a $34 million Series A led by Accel and ICONIQ, the company announced July 23 . Designer Fund also participated, alongside a notably specific angel roster: WorkOS co-founder Michael Grinich, Lovable co-founder Anton Osika, and individual engineers and designers from both Anthropic and OpenAI.
Founder and CEO Stephen Haney built Paper around a specific bet: as more software gets built with the help of AI coding agents, the traditional design handoff -- a human designer producing static mockups for human engineers to implement one at a time -- breaks down . Paper's design environment is built to work natively alongside agentic coding workflows rather than assuming a purely human-to-human pipeline.
“ Paper's design environment is built to work natively alongside agentic coding workflows rather than assuming a purely human-to-human pipeline. ”
The company's early customer list is unusually strong for a Series A-stage design tool: Ramp, Lovable, Vercel, PostHog, Quartr and Y Combinator are all already using Paper, giving it real validation from companies that are themselves building fast, AI-forward engineering organizations.
The dual-lab angel roster -- engineers and designers from both Anthropic and OpenAI writing personal checks -- is a meaningful signal distinct from the institutional round itself: people building frontier coding-agent capability directly are betting the design layer around those agents is a genuinely open, valuable problem rather than something the labs themselves will simply absorb into their own products.
For design-tooling and developer-tools investors, Paper's bet sits at the intersection of two categories that have each drawn heavy capital independently -- AI coding agents and next-generation design tools -- without much overlap between them so far . The risk is platform risk: if Figma, Framer or the coding-agent platforms themselves build comparable agent-native design capability directly, Paper's differentiation narrows quickly.
